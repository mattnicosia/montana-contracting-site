Montana Contracting builds ground-up custom homes across Rockland, Orange, Westchester, Ulster, Greene and Columbia counties in New York, and Bergen County in New Jersey. We have done it since 1984, on riverfront slopes, wooded ridges, open meadows and lake lots.

Most of our homes start with an architect's drawings and a site that pushes back. A steep grade above the Hudson in Croton-on-Hudson. A roofline that twists to follow a wooded slope. A 4,900-square-foot ranch in Palisades with ceilings that rise from 12 to 18 feet and three wood-burning fireplaces. We work out the structure, the systems and the finishes against the site before work starts, so the house that gets built is the house that was drawn.

We join at schematic design and price the design while it can still change, so the budget and the drawings move together. By the time we sign a guaranteed maximum price, more than 80 priced line items sit behind it.

One of our three principals stays on your project from the first walkthrough to the final punch list. Our superintendents came up as carpenters, and our own crews carry much of the work.

Questions

When should we bring a general contractor onto a custom home?

At schematic design. If you wait until the construction documents are done, the budget is whatever the drawings cost. Joining early lets us price the design while it can still change, which avoids a costly redesign later.

Do you work with our architect?

Yes. Most of our homes are built from an architect's plans. We update the budget as the design develops, so the architect works with real numbers instead of guesses.

Where do you build custom homes?

Rockland, Orange, Westchester, Ulster, Greene and Columbia counties in New York, and Bergen County in New Jersey.

Who will we deal with during the build?

One of our three principals, Joe Montana, Matt Nicosia or Chris Lange, stays your contact from the first walkthrough to the final punch list.
