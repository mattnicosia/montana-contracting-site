We build ground-up custom homes from an architect's drawings. Montana Contracting has done it since 1984, across seven counties in the Hudson Valley and Bergen County, New Jersey.

The sites are rarely easy. The Hudson River House sits on a steep grade above the river in Croton-on-Hudson, and its foundations and drainage were engineered for the slope. At Twisted Ridge, every framing member met the next at a different angle, so we modeled the whole structure before cutting a board.

Some homes are hard because of what goes inside them. The Palisades ranch is 4,900 square feet, with ceilings that climb from 12 to 18 feet, three Stuv wood-burning fireplaces and Warmboard radiant heat under every floor.

We like to start at schematic design. Our first budget runs about 12 line items. By the time we sign a guaranteed maximum price at 90% construction documents, it carries more than 80, priced by the trade partners who will do the work.

All three of our principals stay involved in every project. One of them, Joe Montana, Matt Nicosia or Chris Lange, is your contact from the first walkthrough to the final punch list.

Questions

When should we bring a general contractor in on a custom home?

At schematic design. If you wait for finished construction documents, the budget becomes whatever the drawings cost. Early on, the design can still change to meet the number.

Do you work with our architect?

Most of our homes are built from an architect's plans. We update the budget with every revision, so the architect designs against real numbers.

Where do you build custom homes?

Rockland, Orange, Westchester, Ulster, Greene and Columbia counties in New York, and Bergen County in New Jersey.

Who do we deal with during the build?

One of our three principals: Joe Montana, Matt Nicosia or Chris Lange. That person stays your contact from the first walkthrough to the final punch list.
